RESET 2026-07
=============
date:     2026-07
type:     one-time migration record
scope:    the complete replacement of the playbook tree in July 2026.
          Why it happened, what was removed, and how to reach anything
          that preceded it. This document is historical and is not
          amended; a second reset gets its own file.
baseline: pre-reset playbook at 42298a27ad4abec49aa4dc12976b5c466b62f097

  This file carries a date in its name, against the dateless-filename
  rule, because the date is its SUBJECT. Names go stale when they
  describe a position in a process. A record of a dated event cannot.


WHY

  The playbook was started to do one thing: consolidate an LLM
  workflow -- protocol, most-used prompts, tactics, strategies and
  reusable context -- into a single place, and make tacit and latent
  working knowledge explicit so a chat that has never seen it can
  pick it up.

  By July 2026 it had become the opposite. It was 36 files and about
  84,000 tokens, against its own measured required-read budget of
  6,000 for an IMPL thread. A toolkit built to manage context could
  not fit in one. Roughly 40,000 of those tokens -- half the tree --
  were the record of building the tree. The payload the whole thing
  existed to deliver was about 17,000.

  It had also never been used. Four of the five recorded threads were
  the playbook working on itself. The single time it met a real
  project the result was negative: a design thread received the whole
  archive, never opened the render, and followed the project's
  competing style document instead -- and most of its style checks
  still passed, so the pass rate concealed a render with zero
  influence.

  A careful read in July 2026 surfaced nine internal contradictions.
  The close-artifact filename was stated in three places and was
  wrong in two. render.md contradicted itself about its own output in
  two independent paragraphs. ADR-004 was cited for three different
  propositions in three documents. naming.md cited a commit/STOP
  checklist that did not exist. MANIFEST asserted an accept criterion
  check.sh did not implement. The thread-id counter, defined as
  shared per project, held a collision.

  None of it was written carelessly. There was simply no consumer to
  notice that none of it had ever cost anything. Every finding had
  become a rule because it was found, not because it had hurt.

  The decision was to stop patching, take the decisions that had
  earned their place, and rewrite the tree from scratch -- on the
  ground that git already holds everything a provenance apparatus was
  being built to duplicate.


HOW

  The filter applied was: USED, or NEEDED BY THE NEXT REAL TASK.

  Kept, and carried byte-identical where possible:
    preferences/layers.md, preferences/style-contract.md
      the payload. The tacit knowledge made explicit.
    the six workflow prompts
      standalone by design, methods not authorities.
    scripts/pack-repo.sh
      the one component with a clean record, and untouched here
      precisely because that record is its value.

  Kept, consolidated:
    protocol.md
      absorbs naming.md, thread-protocol.md, kickoff.md, close.md,
      precedence.md and MANIFEST.md. Rules renumbered R1-R12 under a
      hard cap of twelve.
    settled.md
      absorbs the 36-entry ADR record as one line each, keeping only
      the roads not taken, which is the part no rule can carry.

  Added, because it came from USE rather than from reading:
    R10 state the turn, R11 declare absence, and the STATE and
    COMMANDS blocks. The author could not tell, from a thread's
    output, when it was his move, what to run, or whether something
    was missing.

  Added, because the thinking tool already in use deserved capture:
    prompts/find-the-isomorph.md and prompts/survey-the-space.md.

  Added:
    scripts/render.sh, automating the stamp but never the
    composition.

  Removed entirely:
    check.sh, MANIFEST.md, VERSION, the ADR record and its era
    shards, the implementation plans, the refinements file, the load
    classes, required-read lists and token budgets, the terminal-state
    taxonomy, the status vocabulary, supersedes fields, append-only
    requirements, stable-forever ids and the rename ceremony.

  llm_playbook/llm/ was DELETED: close artifacts, handoffs and plans
  describing a playbook that no longer exists, and misleading to any
  future reader who found them. It is at 42298a27, reachable by the
  commands below.

  A SECOND SALVAGE PASS WAS RUN BEFORE THE DELETION LANDED, because
  the first pass had judged files from their summaries rather than
  their contents and had nearly lost refinements.md that way. It found
  three more things, all of which are now in the tree:

    preferences/transport.md   the packer's real usage (-p paste mode,
      -e, -o), the three invariants -- scratch-only, committed-read,
      idempotent -- and the three transport situations. None of it was
      anywhere in the reset tree, and committed-read is the reason a
      render must be committed before it is packed. Now in README
      under TRANSPORT. It also CLOSED an open question this reset had
      recorded as unknown: archive where the chat takes an upload,
      paste where only text crosses.

    ADR-016  one thread at a time, and the three mechanisms that
      depend on it and fail together. Carried into the reset blind.
      Now declared in settled.md under DECLARED ASSUMPTIONS, and it
      matters immediately because worktrees are the stated direction.

    ADR-022  composition happens at pack time -- the exact opposite of
      what this session decided about rendering and packing, decided
      without knowing ADR-022 existed. Recorded as superseded with the
      reason, so a reader finding it in git sees it was overruled
      rather than missed.

  The four close artifacts were checked and confirmed safe to delete:
  their durable content had already been promoted. D5 became
  RF-PLAYBOOK-007; D2 became CONSTRAINT-013.

  ONE FILE WAS RESCUED FROM IT: llm/refinements.md, promoted to
  refinements.md at the root. It is not history. It is the intake for
  accretion rule ONE -- the place a finding waits for its second
  occurrence -- and the first draft of this reset deleted it while
  keeping the rule that depends on it. Reading it before deletion also
  surfaced RF-PLAYBOOK-009, which exposed a hole in R12 as first
  written.

  Result: 17 files, about 30,000 tokens. The ratio of payload to
  machinery inverted from roughly 1:4 to roughly 2:1.


WHAT PREVENTS A REPEAT

  Three mechanisms, all acting at write time, in README under
  ACCRETION: a finding is not a rule until it has cost something
  twice in a real project; changes rewrite a document rather than
  patch it; and protocol.md is capped at twelve rules, where a
  thirteenth must name what it replaces.

  Periodic review was considered and rejected. It needs discipline
  nobody sustains and the reviewer is the person who wrote the thing.

  its place. The full text is one command away above, and reopening
